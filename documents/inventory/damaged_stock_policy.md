# Damaged Stock Policy

## Purpose

Defines how damaged or expired stock is identified, kept separate
from usable inventory, and written off, so financial and inventory
records stay accurate.

## Scope

Applies to all raw materials, packaging, and finished goods.

## Identification

Damaged or expired stock can be identified at two points:

- **During receiving** — as part of the Stock Receiving Procedure's
  quality inspection step, when a delivery first arrives
- **During counting** — as part of a weekly spot-check or the monthly
  full count, when existing stock is found damaged or past its usable
  date

## Quarantine

Once identified, damaged or expired items are physically separated
from good stock immediately, before any further action is taken. This
prevents damaged items from being accidentally used in production or
counted as usable inventory in the meantime.

## Write-Off

Quarantined stock is written off by someone other than the person who
identified it — kept separate from the identifying staff member (the
Inventory Officer or Stock Verifier) for the same reason counting and
system-recording are kept separate elsewhere in these procedures:
independent sign-off catches errors and prevents any single person
from both flagging and clearing stock unchecked.

## Recording

Each write-off is logged with the item, quantity, reason (damaged in
transit, expired, damaged in storage, etc.), the point at which it was
identified (receiving or counting), and the date, so damaged-stock
patterns can be tracked over time — including feeding into the
Supplier Management Policy's review of delivery quality where the
damage originated from a specific supplier.

## Responsibilities

- **Inventory Officer / Stock Verifier**: identifies and quarantines
  damaged or expired stock
- **[Role to be named]**: reviews and approves the write-off,
  independent of whoever identified it
